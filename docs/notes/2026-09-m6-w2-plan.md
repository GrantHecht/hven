## §0. Scope — what "feasibility mode" IS, and the gap W2 closes

The driver ALREADY has two feasibility machines, and W2 builds neither:

- **The elastic tier** (`sqp_driver.h:588-780`; mechanics
  `src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:3766-3897`; construction
  `include/hven/detail/globalization/sqp/elastic.h`). An l1 exact-penalty
  reformulation with one scaled slack COLUMN per row violated at
  `p_ref = clamp(0, box)`, a `rho` ladder `1e2 -> 1e8` at `x10`
  (`elastic.h:47-49`), hot-chained rung to rung, feasible by construction.
- **The restoration phase** (`sqp_driver.h:440-462`; entry
  `src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:2668-2790`), three request sources, one per
  solve, running a nested `SqpDriver` on a `RestorationModel` wrapper.

W2 is therefore **not** "add elastic/restoration": it is **making the IPQP
tier's infeasible-suspect escape reach them with its evidence intact, and
pinning what the fallback may and may not conclude.**

### W2.0 gap list (each claim verified against the code)

1. **"The tier's escape path currently discards the evidence into a cold
   walk." — TRUE.** `certified_feasibility_fallback`
   (`src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:939-962`) records exactly two scalars on the
   history row (`row.ipqp_least_infeasible_primal`,
   `row.ipqp_farkas_corroborated`, :956-957), then `(void)ev; (void)seed;
   return engine.solve(qp, overrides);`. `evidence.least_infeasible_x` — the
   n-vector §6.3 exists to produce (`ipqp_engine.h:775-782`) — is never read.
   Call site: `src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:3692`, the escape branch's only
   entry, inside `case QpMode::kIpm`.
2. **"The elastic tier is reached only through the walk's `kInfeasible`." —
   TRUE.** Sole entry: `if (qs.status == QpStatus::kInfeasible)`
   (`src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:3768`), evaluated AFTER the dispatch. Neither
   kernel can put that status in `qs` — both exports force `kOptimal` (`:666`,
   `:915`); `SsnEngine` sets `kInfeasible` internally
   (`src/qp/ssn_engine.cpp:324,774`) only on `kInfeasibleSuspect`, which routes
   to the walk. So under kIpm: tier escapes -> fallback -> **cold walk** ->
   walk certifies `kInfeasible` -> elastic. The evidence is two solves stale.
3. **"Under kIpm an elastic re-solve may itself go to the walk." — TRUE, and
   understated.** Elastic rungs are **unconditionally** the walk:
   `qs_e = engine_.solve(elastic.qp, seed_elastic, elastic_overrides)`
   (`src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:3794`), documented as structural at
   `sqp_driver.h:1703-1707`. There is no mode dispatch inside the ladder at
   all. Same for SOC and for the restoration sub-solve, whose `ropts.qp_mode`
   is reset to `kWalk` explicitly (`src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:2765`).
4. **"The restoration phase does not consume the tier's least-infeasible
   point." — TRUE.** The phase runs `sub.solve(feasibility,
   feasibility.start_point())` (`src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:2783-2784`) — the
   2-arg, cold overload, from the wrapper's own start point derived from the
   current `x`. No seeding surface exists to hand it another point.

Two further gaps found while verifying, both W2's to settle:

5. **The K=3 retirement tally is charged BEFORE the fallback runs.**
   `ladder_outcome = ... : IpqpLadderOutcome::kEscape` and
   `ipqp_ladder.record(...)` at `src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:3569-3574` sit
   above the routing branch, so a W2 fallback that SOLVES the subproblem still
   charges the tier one escape toward retirement (§6.1) and drops the warm
   carry (`:3580`). **RULED (Q-S3, option (a)):** the charge stands for BOTH
   fates — a disproved suspicion is evidence that the tier's DETECTOR is
   unsuited to this problem, so charging it is correct, not an oversight. W2
   pins it as a decision. **Registered for M7:** on a disproval, rung A's
   closed-slack solution is a certified solution with carried multipliers while
   the tier's warm carry was already dropped at `:3580` — recoverable state
   thrown away, worth one M7 task.
6. **The elastic seed surface cannot take a raw point — and by contract must
   not (amendment B).** `elastic_seed(const ElasticQp &, const QpSolution &)`
   (`elastic.h:185`) needs a `QpSolution`, i.e. bound states and an active set,
   while `least_infeasible_x` is a bare `Vec`. v1 read this as a missing
   surface; the review corrects it to a CONTRACT: `seed.x` is the engine's
   window CENTER and is zeroed on every rung
   (`src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:3843-3845`; the WARM SEEDING note in
   `sqp_driver.h`), so a raw point cannot be a seed no matter what surface is
   added. What a seed carries is a WORKING SET — see T2(b).

## §2. The certified fallback CONTRACT

**Input.** `(qp, ev, seed, evidence, overrides, row)` as landed
(`src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:939-942`). The decision reads `evidence` only;
`ev`/`seed` remain available for seeding, never for judgment (pin P7).

**The decision.** A **bounded two-rung attempt ladder** — not a
straight-to-elastic jump, not a cold walk first.
- **Rung A — the elastic QP, always**, via `run_elastic_ladder` (T1). Feasible
  by construction (`sqp_driver.h:611-616`), so the question is answered by
  solving something. Entered at T2's `rho_0` and T2's working-set seed. No
  "weak evidence" branch skips it: the escape already fired, and the elastic
  solve is the cheapest thing that can DISPROVE it (zero slacks => the original
  QP was feasible and the suspicion false, `sqp_driver.h:617-624`).
- **Rung B — the cold walk, only on rung A's non-`kOptimal` exit** (the engine
  can still decline a feasible problem, `sqp_driver.h:611-613`). This is
  today's body verbatim, and the refusal path's carrier.
- **No third rung**: restoration is the driver's tier, not this function's.

**Output vocabulary.** A `QpSolution` in the ORIGINAL variables via
`elastic_project(..., carry_multipliers = closed)` (`elastic.h:216`), PLUS the
`std::optional<ElasticLadderReport>` the fallback attaches (beside `qs`, or on
`row`). `:3768` then becomes two lines, and everything under it is untouched:

```cpp
const ElasticLadderReport report =
    fallback_report ? *fallback_report
                    : run_elastic_ladder(engine_, qp, window, opts_, out.counters);
// ... today's consequence code, unchanged (history row, enter_restoration, project)
```

| rung A outcome | returned status | driver consequence |
|---|---|---|
| slacks closed (`l1 <= feas_tol`) | `kOptimal`, multipliers carried | ordinary funnel judgment; the suspicion is **disproved** |
| slacks open but `reduced` or `promises_f` | `kOptimal`, multipliers ZEROED | ordinary judgment on a relaxed step (today's `elastic_project` rule) |
| ladder exhausted, relaxation still open | `kInfeasible` **+ the report attached** | `:3768` CONSUMES the report — the history row and `enter_restoration()` run off the ladder that already ran; no second ladder |
| rung A not `kOptimal` at all | rung B's walk solution verbatim | today's behavior (refusal) |

**One ladder implementation, one judge, position 5 unmoved** (amendment A,
resolving Q-O1/Q-S1 to (a) in its typed form). The routing chain's escape
branch is still spec §2.3 item 5's single entry; the elastic tier is still the
only judge of an elastic solve. W2 changes where the ladder is ENTERED FROM,
never how many exist — and it does so with a typed report rather than a
sentinel bool, so "which ladder judged this" is a value, not an inference.

**Why option (b) is worse than v1 said** (amendment A): had the fallback
withheld its `kInfeasible` and returned rung B's cold walk instead, that walk
would ITSELF certify `kInfeasible` on a genuinely inconsistent linearization,
so `:3768` runs a ladder on top of the one the fallback already ran and the
walk's own certify pass. Option (b) is three ladders, not v1's two.

**The REFUSAL rule.** The fallback refuses when rung A did not reach
`kOptimal` and rung B's walk did not either, and it refuses by **returning rung
B's solution unchanged** — never by inventing a status, and with NO report
attached. The routing chain then behaves exactly as today: `qs` carries the
walk's status into `:3768`/`:3910`'s failure routing, so a refusal costs a
radius shrink and a retry, never an abort. **No suspicion is ever promoted**:
the only `kInfeasible` this function can produce is the EXHAUSTED-ELASTIC one —
the elastic ladder's certificate class, not the barrier kernel's signature.

**The pins that make it a contract** (all directly callable, no driver):

- **(P1)** evidence in => the elastic ladder ran at
  `max(kElasticRhoInit, dual_norm_start)`.
- **(P2)** closed slacks => `kOptimal`, multipliers carried, `s == 0`.
- **(P3)** exhausted => `kInfeasible`, a report attached, and
  `elastic_activations`/`elastic_escalations` charged exactly once.
- **(P4)** rung A refused => **counters-identical to W1, not byte-identical**
  (amendment C): the same inputs give the same status, a solution identical to
  the QP tolerance, and a rung-B solve whose own `minor_iters`,
  `factorizations` and `symbolic_analyses` equal W1's return on the same
  fixture. Byte-identity is not a valid claim here — W1's D0 leakage finding
  showed a solve's exact bytes can move on state the call does not own (cited
  from the review lane; UNVERIFIED in this draft).
- **(P5)** a default-constructed (`fired == false`) evidence block => rung B
  directly, i.e. the W1 body: a caller with no evidence gets W1's behavior.
- **(P6)** the function never throws on any evidence content.
- **(P7)** **the VERDICT reads NOTHING from the evidence** (amendment E).
  `evidence.fired` selects the P5 branch, `least_infeasible_x` (plus the duals
  of T2(b)) seeds, `dual_norm_start` sets `rho_0` — and nothing else is read.
  Pinned BY MUTATION: flipping `farkas_corroborated`, `exhaustion_route`,
  `primal_start`/`primal_end`/`primal_improvement` or `dual_growth` leaves
  every output identical, `rho_0` excepted where `dual_norm_start` itself
  moves. This is what keeps §6.3's signature from becoming a certificate by
  the back door.

## §3. Amendment C — who solves the elastic QP

**The elastic QP is already a plain `QpProblem`** with `n + ns` variables, the
same `me`/`mi` (`elastic.h:64-69`; `sqp_driver.h:606-608`), and
`IpqpEngine::solve(const QpProblem&, const IpqpSeed*, const IpqpOptions&,
const SolveOverrides&)` (`ipqp_engine.h:1033`) takes exactly that, validated
at the boundary. **So no engine-side elastic form is needed to let the tier
consume it.**

**Recommendation: rung A runs on the WALK in W2** (`QpEngine`) — Amendment C
realized as "the elastic QP is the QP-level answer", not "the IPQP tier solves
it". Reasons, by weight: (1) **the symbolic-analysis pin** — the tier hoists
one analysis per SQP solve keyed on the pattern (`ipqp_engine.h:948-955`; A9
pins `analyze_count == 1`), and the elastic QP's dimension and pattern differ,
so a tier-solved rung A forces a second analysis and breaks or re-states A9,
on the rescue path; (2) **the augmented `H` is singular on the `ns` slack
columns** — IP-PMM's proximal `rho` covers it, but that is exactly the regime
§2.2's inertia machinery was hardest to settle (T4b's fix rounds), and rung A
must be the RELIABLE tier; (3) **zero new surface** — the walk already
hot-chains rung to rung (`src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:3833-3846`, 1
factorization vs 7); (4) the IPQP option's only advantage is gap 6, which T2
pays off once for both kernels.

**What it costs to defer:** if a corpus cell later shows the walk timing out
on a large elastic QP, the tier-solved rung is a small follow-on (the problem
type already fits) — REGISTER it for M7 rather than build it now.

## §4. Restoration interaction, and what "certify infeasibility" means

- **The seed** (amendment F). T4 starts the phase at
  `clamp(x + p_elastic, wrapper box)`, from the report's exhausted rung —
  `p_elastic` is a STEP in the original variables (`:3858-3859`), so it is
  added to `x`, never used as a point. Degrades to
  `feasibility.start_point()` when the report is absent or the sum is
  non-finite; `SqpIterate::restoration_seed_used` records which happened. By
  the time restoration is requested, `x + p_elastic` is the best-known
  low-infeasibility point in the ORIGINAL variables, and the phase's cost is
  dominated by where it starts (`src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:2775-2779`:
  35 majors vs 4, a different cause but the same sensitivity). **Mode-independent**
  (owner ruling Q-O3), by the choice argued at T4.
- **The division of certificates is UNCHANGED and is the contract:**
  - the **barrier tier** produces a *signature* only, never `kInfeasible`
    (spec §6.3; `ipqp_result_to_qp_solution` forces `kOptimal`, `:915`);
  - the **elastic ladder** produces the QP-level certificate — "no admissible
    step reduces the linearized violation" — and is the only `kInfeasible`
    consumer/producer at the driver layer (`:3768`, `sqp_driver.h:1701`);
  - the **restoration phase** produces the NLP-level answer: a subgradient
    certificate of local infeasibility on its `kOptimal` arm
    (`src/drivers/sqp_driver.cpp:2952-2960`, `sqp_types.h`'s SqpSolution note).
  W2 adds a producer to the middle row and a seed to the third; it adds no new
  certificate class and moves no existing one.
