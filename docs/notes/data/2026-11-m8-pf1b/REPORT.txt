M8 PF-1b -- the release re-admission rule after PF-1: the suspect ladder, the rung's size, the sibling cell
================================================================================
Base 7a95a612 (origin/m8, PF-1 + riders). Every number below was measured on this machine under the box lock
(PROVENANCE.txt). The instruments are a scratch diagnostic build of 7a95a612 that is never committed (tools/: the
PF-1 D4QP dump patch, an env-tunable copy of the rule with a re-admission tracer, and a replay tool that runs a dump
under the walk seam's observer, recomputes the KKT residuals of the returned point from the QP's data, and solves the
UNREGULARIZED EQP of the returned working set with Eigen's SparseLU). Results are in results/, the per-minor CSVs
of the key runs and the n2000 QP1 dump in data/.

VERDICT: NO ENGINE CHANGE. Every QP that takes the rung reaches an exact KKT point when allowed to finish,
through a certified exit whose own factorization is clean. The sibling cell is a budget-bound walk, not a cycle,
and a wider trigger makes it worse. The rung lands ~1.5 decades below the backend's static-pivot floor, and a
halving rung measured better on the one face that needs it; that is a reason to redesign the regularization
(proximal centring, registered below), not to re-tune a constant on one sample. One comment was false and is
corrected (10ac5642, include/hven/detail/qp/qp_engine.h, comment only; libhven.a objects 56/56 byte-identical to
7a95a612: results/object-identity.log).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(a) THE SUSPECT LADDER, AND WHETHER THE ANSWER IS TRUSTWORTHY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why the ladder does not fire: BY DESIGN. The walk's suspect ladder (one decade of primal_delta per rung, at most 3)
answers exactly two events (qp_engine.cpp, section 4b): (i) an EQP answer the EQP-site residual check REFUSES,
drifts off met rows, or is non-finite; (ii) a would-be-kOptimal exit off a kSuspect factorization whose free-block
stationarity check fails. A kSuspect verdict on an ordinary pass is deliberately not a trigger (header: "kSuspect
is deliberately NOT a repair trigger"). Measured on the PF-1 fixture (results/exp-a/head.txt,
results/summary-n1000.txt):
after the rung (minor 313, dual_mu 1e-8 -> 1e-10) 1443 passes read kSuspect (2394 perturbed pivots, at most 6 in a
pass); the EQP-site check refused nothing -- the perturbed passes' row residuals against their own regularized
system are median 5.7e-14, max 3.0e-10, against a 1e-1 refusal tolerance -- and the terminal pass read kOk with 0
perturbed pivots. Neither trigger is reached, so the ladder is silent, as designed.
It would also be the wrong answer: the perturbed pivots are DUAL-caused (mu pushed below Pardiso's static-pivot
threshold; iparm[9] = 8, eps = 1e-8, in the iparm dump of record), and a primal_delta rung does not clear a
dual-caused perturbed pivot -- hven's own IPQP engine records the same fact and re-routes such reports to its DUAL
shift (ipqp_engine.h, kIpqpPivotReroutePrimalMax). So the silence is not a missed failure. The one gap was a false
contract sentence: 7a95a612's comment on kDualRegDecreaseFactor said a face the lowered mu no longer resolves "is
caught by the inertia verdict (kSuspect on perturbed pivots) and the suspect ladder". The ladder catches nothing
there; the certified exit's gates are what guard the result. Corrected (comment only).
Perturbation vs depth (rungs off, dual_mu fixed from the start, the PF-1 fixture, results/exp-a):

Is the answer trustworthy: YES, here, and the evidence is independent of every factorization the walk took.
  * H is positive definite (SimplicialLDLT of H: D in [7.5e-4, 1.6e-3], no non-positive pivot), so the QP is
    strictly convex and its KKT point is the unique global minimizer.
  * The returned point's KKT residuals, recomputed from the QP's data (tests/qp/support/qp_dump.h, qp_dump_kkt):
    equality 2.8e-17, inequality 6.9e-18, box 0, stationarity 1.8e-18, wrong-sign multipliers 0, complementarity
    3.3e-20 (results/exp-a/head.txt, PF1K).
  * The UNREGULARIZED EQP of the returned working set (5000 free columns, 3097 rows), solved by SparseLU with three
    refinement steps (relative residual 2.8e-17), lands 6.3e-15 from the returned x and 6.4e-15 from its
    multipliers; 0 negative row multipliers; no inactive row violated (PF1E).
  * Every dual_mu in the table above, and every rung variant below, reaches the same q.
  The same holds on the sibling cell's QP1 run to completion (part c): stationarity 3.0e-18, sign 0, compl 1.9e-20,
  exact EQP 1.4e-14 / 1.7e-14 away, after 7497 kSuspect passes and 58712 perturbed pivots.
  Why the perturbed path does not matter here: perturbation is confined to the walk's large, ill-conditioned
  middle (n1000: none once the working set is below ~370 members; results/summary-n1000.txt) and the certified
  exit's own factorization is clean (kOk, 0 perturbed pivots) on every run, n1000 and n2000 alike. The walk only
  uses a perturbed answer as a search direction; the certificate is taken on a clean factor, then polished mu-free.
Accelerate: UNOBSERVED. Accelerate reports no perturbed-pivot count (the verdict cannot read kSuspect from
  perturbation there), and its default LDL^T uses threshold partial pivoting rather than static perturbation (not
  observed here), so what the walk does below the floor there is not inferable from these runs. Pass A of the syntax sweep covers compilation only.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(b) THE RUNG'S SIZE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Measured (results/exp-a, results/exp-b; the rung at minor 312 in every variant, row 905):
    rung                 rungs fired  mu after  status    facts  kSuspect passes  perturbed pivots
    x1e-2, max 2 (head)       1        1e-10    kOptimal  3588        1443             2394
    x1e-1, max 1/2/4          1        1e-9     kOptimal  3631         880              919
    x0.3,  max 2              1        3e-9     kOptimal  3637          29               29
    x0.5,  max 1              1        5e-9     kOptimal  3640          11               11
    x0.5,  max 2/4            2        2.5e-9   kOptimal  3641          11               11
  All five reach the same exact optimum. The face the walk cycles on (rungs off, 400 minors; PF1S): the working
  set's constraint block A_f (3835 x 4763) has sigma_max 2.24 and sigma_min 2.55e-6, so sigma_min^2 = 6.5e-12 and
  dual_mu = 1e-8 exceeds it by ~1500x. Note what the table says about the mechanism: a HALVING breaks the cycle
  although 5e-9 still exceeds sigma_min^2 by ~770x -- the false fixed point is fragile, it is not "mu must resolve
  sigma_min^2". The rung needs to move the regularized fixed point, not to reach the face's conditioning.
What a state-of-the-art policy does (published literature only):
  1. Keep the regularization above the factorization's stability floor. A quasidefinite K = [H+dI A'; A -mI] is
     stably factorized in a fixed pivot order only while d and m are not too small relative to ||K|| and the unit
     roundoff (Gill, Saunders and Shinnerl, SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 17(1), 1996; Saunders, "Cholesky-based
     methods for sparse least squares: the benefits of regularization", 1996; Saunders and Tomlin, SOL 96-4, 1996).
     Below the floor a static-pivoting factorization (Li and Demmel, SC'98; Schenk and Gartner, ETNA 23, 2006 --
     Pardiso's own scheme, whose default 1e-8 is ~sqrt(eps_mach)) replaces the small pivots with eps*||A|| and
     relies on iterative refinement: the effective regularization is then the backend's, not the caller's. That is
     what the 192 / 2394 perturbed pivots are.
  2. Regularize on demand, not by schedule. Ipopt applies its constraint regularization delta_c only when the
     factor reports a singular matrix and walks delta_w down geometrically between iterations (Wachter and
     Biegler, Math. Program. 106, 2006, section 3.1); Altman and Gondzio (Optim. Methods Softw. 11-12, 1999)
     regularize dynamically, per pivot, only the pivots that become dangerously small.
  3. Remove the bias rather than shrink it. The fixed point exists because the dual regularization is centred at
     0. Centred at the current multiplier estimate (the proximal method of multipliers), the regularized and the
     exact solutions coincide at any fixed point, at a regularization that stays bounded away from zero:
     Friedlander and Orban (Math. Prog. Comp. 4, 2012), Pougkakiotis and Gondzio (Comput. Optim. Appl. 78, 2021);
     in SQP form, stabilized SQP's -mu(y - y_E) (Wright, COAP 11, 1998; Hager, COAP 12, 1999; Gill and Robinson,
     SIAM J. Optim. 23, 2013); in active-set QP form, the regularized methods of Gill and Wong (Math. Prog. Comp. 7,
     2015). Iterative refinement against the unregularized system (OSQP's polish: Stellato et al., Math. Prog.
     Comp. 12, 2020) cannot replace it on such a face: its contraction is ~mu/(mu + sigma_min^2), ~1 here, which is
     why PF-1 measured the polish not breaking the cycle.
  So: a fixed x1e-2 rung is neither (1) nor (3). The SOTA answer is (3) -- the dual twin of the proximal primal
  centring hven already adopted (M7 W2 E3/E4) -- and with it the rung retires. If a rung is kept until then, the
  measured bound is (1): one halving, which clears this face with 11 perturbed pivots instead of 2394.
Why no change now: the rule's outcome is correct on every measured case; the corpus holds ONE face where the rung
matters (f7_n1000_path_corrupted QP1; the other cell that takes it is unaffected by it, part c), so a re-sized
rung would be a constant tuned on one sample, and a halving rung's sufficiency on any other face is unmeasured.
The structural fix carries its own acceptance fixture: this face, at dual_mu = 1e-8, without a rung.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(c) THE SIBLING CELL, f7_n2000_path_corrupted
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The cell, re-run with dumps on the diagnostic build, reproduces the census row exactly (WorkLimit, 59746 / 60000,
QPs 19873;20001;19872; results/cell-n2000/). Engine solves 0-5 are the corrupted start's SETUP solve at p0 (not in
the row's counters; solves 3 and 4 of it also run to their 20000-minor caps, without any rung); solves 6/7/8 are
the designated hop's QP1/QP2/QP3 (n 10000, me 6000, mi 2000), each at the derived minor cap 20000 (the dumps' opts
line).
QP1 is NOT a cycle. Replayed (results/exp-c1, reproduces 20000 minors / 19873 factorizations, the rung at minor
5337 on row 1476): minors 0-3000 make ~3000 adds from the corrupted start (objective 0 -> 0.45, working set 1188 ->
3828); from ~4400 the objective falls window by window and the working set shrinks steadily, 3585 at minor 5000 ->
712 at the cap (~170 members per 1000 minors late on). The descent starts ~900 minors BEFORE the rung and goes on
at the same kind of rate after it. Run to completion (results/exp-c2/qp6_full):
kOptimal at 23431 minors / 22643 factorizations, an exact KKT point (above). Rung off: the same cap, objective
0.0390 at it against 0.0383 with the rung (results/exp-c2/qp6_norung) -- the rung is immaterial to this QP. The
PF-1 census's "+1 factorization" is the discarded factor state, nothing more.
The trigger's narrowness: the tracer logs every refresh re-admission of a row released earlier in the solve.
QP1's are 39, all between minors 4308 and 4664 and all same-pass (released and re-admitted within one pass, not
by a drop), plus the one at 5337 that the rule takes. The sampled ones are the add site's exchange: the pass adds
its blocker, exchanges a working row out, and the refresh shift-adds that row back (pass net +1). None is the
"delayed by an intervening blocked pass" case. Widening the trigger to every such re-admission (HVEN_PF1B_WIDE,
the reviewer's per-row "released since the last KKT point" hypothesis in its broadest form): the
n1000 fixture is unchanged (4758 / 3588), and n2000 QP1 takes two rungs (4308, 4377 -> dual_mu 1e-12), still
caps, ends HIGHER (q 0.0486 against 0.0383) and reads 131450 perturbed pivots against 58137. Refuted for this
cell, and harmful.
QP2 and QP3 are the SAME subproblem as QP1 (H, g, A, b, bounds and seed_x identical in the dumps); the driver's
QP-failure retry shrinks the trust region, 1 -> 0.5 -> 0.25, and the seeds' working-set flags differ (1186 and 584
of 12000 entries).
  QP2 (TR 0.5; results/exp-c3/qp7): run to completion, kOptimal at 24338 minors / 23603 factorizations, the SAME
  optimum as QP1 (q = -3.534059099469761e-03, TR inactive there), exact KKT (stat 2.5e-18, EQP 1.0e-14 away), no
  rung, no re-admission after minor 3277, no perturbed pivot. Another budget-bound walk.
  QP3 (TR 0.25; results/exp-c4/qp8, 30000 minors; a run to 200000 was stopped by me at ~44 min): a THIRD shape.
  From minor ~4300 every pass is a zero-length bound add at the full count (working set 4000 = n - me, 3121 of the
  pins TR pins), the iterate never becomes equality-feasible (eq residual 1.6e-2, inequality 1.3e-2 at minor
  30000), and the objective creeps (0.06315 -> 0.06447). No rung, no re-admission after minor 3910, no perturbed
  pivot. A degenerate-vertex exchange stall on a TR-restricted subproblem that may be infeasible (unverified: no
  LP oracle was run) -- an infeasibility-detection / anti-cycling item, not a regularization one.
What would move the cell: not regularization. QP1 and QP2 need 23431 and 24338 minors against a cap of 20000, and
19873 of QP1's 20000 minors refactorize (the Schur border is taken 44 times); retrying a budget-bound QP with a
smaller trust region spends a full cap for nothing (QP2) or leads into QP3's stall. Registered below.

    effect on n1000, harmful on n2000. Not adopted.

    factorization, an asserted column), so no other corpus walk took the rung. Unmeasured; it
    stays registered, not adopted.

    (the census row cannot show the rung today). A public-API addition; registered for the M8 solver-API revisit
    rather than a standalone chain.
