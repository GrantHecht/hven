READING
- Rule A is clean by the round's criteria (no worse status, no lost certified cell, the qp26 route held: kOptimal, 23 minors,
  the escalation fires there exactly as designed), and it keeps the scale class won (ALLINITC.K, F3, STEENBRB.D cost).
- Rule A does NOT win CRESC100.K. The P retakes there (n=205 with 100 perturbed pivots; n=149 with 43) answer the EQP's rhs
  with eta 2.3e-18 and 6.4e-18 and the EQP-site residual check ACCEPTS them (ratio 3.9e-2 and 2.8e-6), so neither clause of the
  rule fires; the answers are huge (||y|| 7.5e13 and 1.7e10 against the platform answer's 8.2e7 and 4.1e12).
- Rule B wins CRESC100.K but sends ALLINITC.K back to numerical_error (the scale-class cell whose P retake also keeps perturbed
  pivots but whose P answer is the good one; M alone loses it, as round v3's nsM arm did). Neither rule wins both classes.

WHY THE ENGINE REFUSES AN ANSWER WHOSE BACKWARD ERROR IS 1e-7 (qp26, minor 15)
  platform numerics: 1 perturbed pivot, eta 0.251, ||r|| 1.5e36, ||y|| 3.3e35          -> trigger fires, P retake
  P numerics:        5 perturbed pivots, eta 1.07e-7, ||K_D|| 3.98, ||y/D|| 1.26e55, ||r_D|| 5.35e48, ||b_D|| 1.0
  EQP-site check:    constraint-row residual 1.15e49 against the scale 0.1 (1 + ||b_rows|| = 0.403): ratio 3.72e7 -> refused
The two checks measure different things. The normwise backward error eta = ||r|| / (||K|| ||y|| + ||b||) asks whether y solves
SOME nearby system; its denominator carries ||y||, so an answer blown up along the near-null direction a static pivot
perturbation (1e-8 ||K||) leaves -- here to 1e55 -- reads as "backward stable" however large its residual. The engine's check is
a residual relative to the right-hand side (the constraint rows against 0.1 (1 + ||b_rows||)), the forward-looking question the
walk needs answered, and it sees the 1e49 residual. Neither is wrong; they answer different questions, and the
perturbed-pivot regime is exactly where they disagree. That is why the escalation has to count the engine's refusal (rule A),
and why the TRIGGER on the platform's own numerics can stay on eta: there the blow-up shows up in eta too (0.251 at qp26).
On CRESC100 the P answers pass BOTH (eta 1e-18, ratio 0.039): no residual-based test separates them, which is what leaves the
pairing class open under rule A.
